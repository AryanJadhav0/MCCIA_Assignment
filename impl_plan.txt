What has been implementedPhase 1: Data foundation (D1)Loaded the six files and ran 30 integrity checks: unique keys, PO to receipt to payment joins (all 1:1), date order, received not above ordered, rejection not above received, billing equals ordered quantity x quoted price. All pass.Built a 4,800-row PO fact table: short quantity, days late, billing gap, baseline and excess gap, rejection value, price vs panel and vs index, in-master tag, Arora's payment lateness.Parsed the master category list into a supplier x category map.Findings logged: data is clean (no duplicates, nulls or unit mix-ups); 87% of POs are for materials outside the supplier's master categories; the market index covers 6 of 18 materials and PO prices sit about 13.5% above it.Phase 2: Returns attribution (35% of the judging weight)Model: Bayesian, supplier return rate x volume, times material and quantity likelihoods. The high-return-rate group is learned from the data, not from the underperformer flag.Validation: 5 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation on the 240 known returns, with seven model variants compared as an ablation.Results: top-1 accuracy 22% and top-3 55%, against 4% / 22% for 'latest receipt wins'. Aggregate counts recovered closely (VS12: about 51 expected vs 54 actual).Date proximity tested and rejected: median lag 84 days for the true supplier vs 79 for others (p = 0.83), and adding it makes the model worse.Inferred returns valued as probability-weighted expected rupees, with an 80% range from 4,000 simulated assignments.Phase 3: Scorecard (D2)34-supplier scorecard: short delivery %, late delivery (% of POs and average days), rejection %, returns per 1,000 MT, price vs panel (scaled by z-score).Composite 0-100: short 35%, late 25%, rejection 15%, returns 15%, price 10%.
